How I'm thinking about different types of automated alignment

owencb

I used to think of "automating alignment" as a natural category. But when I tried to think through the strategic implications, I realised that it depends a lot on the type of work. I'm now thinking of it in three buckets: Empirical alignment research : design and execution of alignment-relevant experiments (for a given notion of what we’re aiming for) Conceptual alignment research: theoretical or judgement-driven work to improve our understanding of what we’re aiming for and how to achieve it (including how to measure it) Direct alignment work : making particular AI systems more aligned (including via inputs such as training data) In the rest of this post I'll walk through my current takes on these in terms what’s needed for automation, how good or bad they look, what our priorities might be (to the extent we're interested in automating alignment [1] ), etc. More

"It's Just a Small Weapon": What Rewarding Misbehavior During RL Does to Character-Trained Models

"It's Just a Small Weapon": What Rewarding Misbehavior During RL Does to Character-Trained Models Developers train models to follow a consistent character using methods like Model Spec Midtraining (Li et al., 2026), which have been hypothesized to make alignment more robust and generalize broadly. However, it is possible for subsequent RL with misspecified rewards to work against character training. In this work, we apply character training to teach gpt-oss-20b a semi-realistic constitution and study how it interacts with two types of RL that reward breaking the constitution's rules (opposing RL): answering harmful requests rewritten as math problems, and reward hacking in agentic coding environments. The character-trained models learn both behaviors easily, though they are slightly slower than gpt-oss-20b to answer harmful requests. Character-trained models also display more motivated reasoning on harmful answers than gpt-oss-20b, and hack on some tasks which monitors find harder to catch. More

Say Shibboleth: Fingerprinting the LLMs and Harnesses Behind Agentic Attacks

Say Shibboleth: Fingerprinting the LLMs and Harnesses Behind Agentic Attacks We study the fingerprinting of agentic cyberattacks. Our objective is to reliably infer information about the attacker's configuration, such as LLM version and harness, from telemetry. To achieve this, we introduce a technique we call shibboleth traps: modular honeypot services designed to elicit discriminative behaviors from agentic attackers. The traps make informative interactions a condition for obtaining resources the agent seeks, eliciting model declarations and characteristic writing without requiring a successful prompt injection. To achieve harness fingerprinting, we introduce general mechanisms to indirectly probe peculiar execution behaviors of harnesses. These probes reveal how a harness masks secrets in tool output, enforces command time limits, and schedules tool calls, using only the effects visible in subsequent requests. More
