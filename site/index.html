Stuart_Armstrong

Suppose we have an AI CEO, ruthlessly devoted to maximising shareholder value. Fortunately, we haven't been completely stupid, and have given it a rich definition of shareholder value, encompassing the success, flourishing, safety, preference satisfaction, and moral values of the shareholders, not just the stock price. So it won't destroy the company - or the world - to build a giant stock-ticker with a swiftly increasing number on it. This rich definition is contained in a sophisticated LLM, able to analyse the current situation and answer any question about the objective. The LLM is an integral part of the AI's system and motivation, not something it can simply set aside or hack directly. A Simpler and Better Debate Protocol for Scalable Oversight

Zhiyang Xun

Linkpost to arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02557 Summary: One promising approach to scalable oversight is AI debate , which seeks to leverage a debate between two powerful AIs to break complex questions down into simpler claims that can be easily judged directly. Theoretical work on debate aims to design protocols (i.e., rules of the debate game) that provide rigorous guarantees on correctness, and the current best protocol works for all problems that have sufficiently stable decompositions into subproblems. We design a new protocol, FBS Debate, for this same class of problems that improves on the prior work: correctness holds in a worst-case rather than an average-case sense, and for a stronger equilibrium concept. We also prove that no protocol for this class of problems can outperform FBS Debate while treating human judgment as a black box. More

Redwood Research Oct 16, 2026

“Screwtape: Adversarial Transcript Editing for Monitor Red-Teaming” by Gosha Kozhevnikov, Tyler

Redwood Research Blog

Subtitle: Automatically finding monitor failures by editing real agent transcripts. We introduce Screwtape, a pipeline that turns agent transcripts into realistic attacks that evade action-only monitors (monitors that don’t look at the agent's chain of thought) by inserting an attack into a transcript and repeatedly refining it until a monitor no longer catches it. An LLM verifier with access to the edited transcript and an attack description checks whether the attack is realistic, is clearly harmful, and could plausibly succeed in the original environment. We ran the pipeline on Redwood staff transcripts with our own monitors based on Opus 4.8 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, and then tested the resulting attacks against a held-out monitor from a frontier developer. We ran the pipeline 252 times on 94 unique transcripts. This produced 35 attacks that evaded our monitors. More
